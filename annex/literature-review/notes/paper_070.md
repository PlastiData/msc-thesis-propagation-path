## Problem statement

Modern industrial networks incorporating microservices architectures and Industrial Internet of Things devices exhibit exponential complexity growth that complicates anomaly detection and root-cause localization. When failures occur in these environments, they propagate through network dependencies and generate cascading alarms that obscure the actual fault origin. Existing deep learning approaches for fault detection achieve strong performance but lack interpretability, creating a black box problem that undermines trust and delays critical human intervention in infrastructure management. The challenge is to develop a root-cause localization method that maintains high accuracy while providing transparent explanations of how failures propagate through complex industrial topologies.

## Method summary

The proposed Explainable Graph Transformer framework combines spatial and temporal analysis through a graph attention mechanism operating on industrial network topologies. The model represents the network as a graph where nodes correspond to services or devices and edges capture structural dependencies. A spatial-temporal graph attention mechanism processes both the topology structure and time-series metric data from each node to identify anomaly patterns. The key innovation is a causal attention regularization term integrated into the training objective that biases the model toward prioritizing edges representing actual causal failure propagation paths rather than spurious correlations. This regularization forces attention weights to align with causal relationships in the dependency graph. The framework outputs both a root-cause prediction and attention-based visualizations showing which propagation paths the model considers most relevant for explaining the observed failure cascade.

## Ground truth and evaluation

The evaluation uses both synthetic and real-world datasets to assess localization accuracy and interpretability. The paper reports that the approach outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in localization accuracy and demonstrates reduction in mean time to repair. Experiments compare the method against existing baselines though the specific nature of ground truth labeling for root causes is not detailed in the abstract. The evaluation includes assessment of the intuitive visualizations of fault propagation paths generated by the attention mechanism. Performance metrics focus on the accuracy of identifying the true root cause among candidate nodes and the practical impact on repair time in operational settings.

## Gaps this paper opens

The reliance on causal attention regularization raises questions about how ground truth causal relationships are defined and validated in complex industrial settings where causality may be ambiguous or context-dependent. The focus on industrial networks and IIoT devices leaves unclear how the approach generalizes to cloud-native microservices architectures with different failure characteristics and observability patterns. The paper does not address how the explainability mechanism performs when multiple simultaneous root causes exist or when failures exhibit non-deterministic propagation patterns. The integration of domain knowledge into the causal regularization term and the sensitivity of the method to incorrect or incomplete dependency graph specifications remain unexplored. Additionally, the computational overhead of the graph transformer architecture for real-time root-cause localization in large-scale deployments is not characterized.

## Relevance to the thesis topic

This paper is highly relevant to the thesis framework for root cause analysis in cloud-native systems using temporal and dependency analysis. The graph transformer architecture directly addresses the need to model service dependency topologies while the spatial-temporal attention mechanism aligns with the thesis emphasis on temporal analysis of metric data and failure propagation. The causal attention regularization represents a concrete technique for incorporating causality into dependency-based root cause analysis, which is central to the thesis approach. The explainability focus addresses a critical requirement for practical RCA systems where operators need to understand and trust automated diagnoses. While the paper targets industrial networks rather than cloud-native systems specifically, the core challenges of cascading failures in dependency graphs and the need to distinguish root causes from propagated effects are identical. The method provides a concrete instantiation of combining topology and temporal information that can inform the thesis framework design, though adaptation would be needed to handle cloud-native observability data formats and deployment characteristics.
